## Season 1 — battlecode22-vibe

### 1. Training method and how well it worked

Method (`TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md`): "the Gauntlet" = current bot vs every opponent on every map in a
loop set, both sides (`2*B*N` games). Accept gate: >= 60% of *peer* games (opponents we beat
30-90%); *benchmark* opponents (<30%) are tracked but excluded from the gate and played only every
3rd Gauntlet. Every accept is frozen as `src/g_iterN/` (via `tools/snapshot.sh`) and becomes an
opponent; opponents beaten >= 80% in two consecutive Gauntlets are retired. Each iteration follows
Step 4 (pick a losing game) -> Step 5 (hypothesis with pre-registered variables/thresholds, verified
from the replay, plus a generality check on a second loss and an "act-on-it" check against the log)
-> Step 6 (solution; mechanistic verification with three outcomes: won / engaged-but-couldn't-flip
with an evidenced reason / didn't engage; 8-peer reproduction sample; full Gauntlet; game-by-game
diff against the last accepted run "by shape"). Later additions: near-miss refinement (within 5 pts
of 60%), "never idle" + "track functional areas" + a first-class "high-risk structural exploration"
track after 3 consecutive rejects in one area, a mandatory Iteration-0 bytecode monitor, a
replay-archive rule, a round-count secondary metric, and a fixed old-bot roster (every 10th
snapshot) as the absolute-progress yardstick.

Results (`TRAINING_LOG.md` tail, `LEARNINGS.md` header, `progress/`):
- 128 logged iterations, 62 accepted snapshots (`src/g_iter1..g_iter62`) => ~48% accept rate.
- Final peer Gauntlet: 438/720 = 60.8% against a 36-peer roster (i.e. hovering at the gate by
  construction, since the roster keeps hardening). Fixed old-bot roster (`progress/
  vs_old_bots_history.csv`): 100% vs g_iter1, 80% vs g_iter11, 55% vs g_iter21, ~50% vs g_iter41/51.
- Benchmarks (real 2022 finalists vendored at Iteration 7): `sample_camelcase` 0/20 for essentially
  the whole project (one win, Iteration 28); `sample_afinals` plateaued ~3/20 (15%). The bot never
  reached tournament-finalist strength.
- Best single-iteration peer scores: Iteration 28 (78.1%, targeting priority), 35 (60.3->69.7%,
  sticky miner target), 61 (65.0%), 86 (64.2%), 64 (63.8%).
- A/B side asymmetry at the end: 27.8pt -> 14.2pt after Iteration 126; `chessboard`/`squer` still
  sharply lopsided. Live bytecode monitor found 2 confirmed overruns in 720 games (Iteration 127).

### 2. What worked / what did not

Worked (with measurements):
1. Target priority combat units > structures > workers instead of "always chase the Archon"
   (Iteration 28): peer 78.1%, pillars 45%->81%, first-ever benchmark win.
2. Sticky committed target instead of recomputing "nearest" each round (Iteration 35): 60.3->69.7%;
   found by reading individual `--moves` traces (Miner ping-ponging between two tiles), not
   aggregates.
3. Live census via per-round accumulator + first-actor-publishes-last-round (Iterations 11-12);
   cumulative-ever counters had silently broken build rules. `src/bot/RobotPlayer.java:census()`.
4. Shared focus-fire slot + retreat-to-heal + reinforce-the-live-fight landed as one package
   (Iteration 12) after four isolated attempts (8-11) were individually inert.
5. Raising retreat threshold from "any damage" to "critical HP only" (Iteration 18); matches
   cross-year postmortem finding in `RESEARCH.md` section 4.
6. One cheap turn-1 latch (`richHome`: lead sensed near spawn > 600) reused as the branch for
   Miner quota, Builder caps, Watchtower/Lab investment — most-reused conditional in the codebase.
7. Escalating-threshold discretionary spending (1000 -> 2500 -> 5000 lead per extra structure),
   proven across Iterations 117/118/122/123/125.
8. Self-calibrating threshold derived from in-game event counts (Iteration 73) after four fixed-
   constant variants each traded one opponent's gain for another's loss.
9. Exploration momentum with a strict, short stuck detector (Iteration 86 v3: <2 tiles in 10
   rounds): 64.2%; v1/v2 (looser windows) regressed.
10. Symmetric tie-breaking (Iteration 126 shipped): all fixed-compass `Direction[]` orderings and
    "first sensed result wins" sites replaced with map-center/target-relative tiebreaks; A/B gap
    halved *and* overall win rate rose (61.0% vs 57.5% baseline). Also the mirror-match check
    (bot vs byte-identical copy) as the detector for this class.

Did not work (with measurements):
1. Fixing asymmetry with random or per-robot-random tiebreaks (Iteration 126 v1/v2, Iteration 67):
   fairer but net regression — a consistent arbitrary preference gave army cohesion.
2. Any gate on "no enemy contact recently" (Miner quota, Watchtower gate, Sage gate): in a real war
   the flag never clears, so the gate is a permanent off-switch (Iterations 41, 114, 115).
3. The 23-iteration multi-Archon starvation thread (39-61): first ~10 iterations fixed *income*
   and provably never moved the starved Archon's build count; the real cause was turn-order
   dominance over a shared pool.
4. Putting a new structure on the producer's own build ring (Iteration 57): 9750 lead unspent,
   build queue paralyzed. Tile occupancy, not affordability, gated `canBuildRobot()`.
5. Trusting an 8-peer sample: one change looked clean at 8 peers and had a 6-flip single-map
   regression at full scale (build/priority/threshold changes are the risky category).
6. Genuine terrain-based symmetry detection scout (Iteration 88): mechanically correct, net
   negative — the scout's cost exceeded the value on this ruleset. Blind rotational assumption
   (Iteration 76) was wrong on 4/10 maps.
7. Sage vision-gap kiting (Iteration 89): correct exploit, zero effect, because the gold economy
   never produced a Sage — "does the feature ever engage?" was never checked. Worked once re-tried
   after the upstream economy existed (Iteration 116).
8. "Helps the diagnosed case, hurts broadly" shape: Archon repair throttle (63/74/75), distress
   reinforcement (75), miner rescue (69-72). The triggering condition fired far more often across
   other games than the one replay suggested.
9. Chasing map-fragile flips (`chessboard`, `squer`, `sandwich`, `maptestsmall`, `pillars`): a
   persistent g_iter21/chessboard flip was traced and had no single cause — emergent from ~90
   iterations of timing drift.
10. Away-from-home exploration bias (Iteration 65, severe regression) and multi-Archon threat
    severity (Iteration 128: byte-identical trajectory on the motivating game — never engaged).

How `bc22_replay.py` parses replays (relevant because `.bc24` is the same format family —
gzipped FlatBuffers, root `GameWrapper`, `events: [EventWrapper]` with a union `Event`, and rounds
stored as **parallel flat vectors**; the per-Turn action-union layout only arrived in later years):
- `load()` reads bytes, detects gzip by the `1f 8b` magic, `gzip.decompress`, then
  `S.GameWrapper.GetRootAs(raw, 0)`.
- Iterates `gw.Events(i)`; `ev.EType()` selects the union member; the member is materialized with
  a tiny helper: `inner = ev.E(); obj = Cls(); obj.Init(inner.Bytes, inner.Pos)` (this is how the
  flatc Python API wraps a union table). `GameHeader` -> `MatchHeader` -> N x `Round` ->
  `MatchFooter` are collected into a `Match` object; multi-match files are supported.
- `MatchHeader.Map()` gives name, min/max corner, `Symmetry()` (0 rot / 1 horiz / 2 vert),
  `RandomSeed()`, terrain vectors and `Bodies()` (initial units); `GameHeader.BodyTypeMetadata`
  gives every unit's hp/dmg/cooldowns/radii/bytecode limit, printed as a table (this is how
  several engine facts were confirmed without decompiling).
- Per `Round`: `TeamIds/TeamLeadChanges/TeamGoldChanges` (resource deltas), `SpawnedBodies()`
  (table of parallel vectors; **absent on rounds with no spawns**, so null-check), `MovedIds()` +
  `MovedLocs()` (a `VecTable` of `Xs/Ys`), `ActionIds/Actions/ActionTargets` (byte enum + int
  target; `CHANGE_HEALTH` targets carry the HP delta), `DiedIds()`, `IndicatorStringIds()` +
  `IndicatorStrings()`. All coordinates are shifted by the map's min corner.
- State is simulated forward (robot dict keyed by id -> team/type/x/y/hp; team resource totals as
  running sums; live terrain grids) and rendered every `--step` rounds. Identical
  (actor,action,target) triples in a round are collapsed to "xN".
- Gotchas recorded: buildings' prototype HP is not modelled; round-1 resource delta includes the
  starting stipend; periodic resource regen is *not* streamed and had to be recomputed from
  `GameHeader.Constants`.
For 2024: fetch `schema/battlecode.fbs` from the `battlecode/battlecode24` repo, regenerate with
`flatc --python --gen-onefile`, keep `load()`/`event_table()` verbatim, and rewrite the enum tables
and the per-round field names against the new `Round` table (expect the same parallel-vector
shape plus 2024-specific vectors such as traps/flags). Keep the synthetic-replay test approach.

### 4. Bot architecture patterns worth reusing (`src/bot/RobotPlayer.java`, 1772 lines; `Dijkstra20.java`, 2388 lines)

- Main loop (`run()`, lines ~170-235): capture `roundAtStart`, sense enemies once, update shared
  signals, dispatch by type, `catch GameActionException` and `catch Exception` separately, and a
  `finally { checkBytecodeBudget(rc, roundAtStart); Clock.yield(); }`.
- Bytecode monitor (`checkBytecodeBudget`, ~line 247): overrun = `rc.getRoundNum() != roundAtStart`
  at the `finally` (the limiter pauses mid-instruction and resumes next round — no exception);
  near-miss = `Clock.getBytecodeNum() >= 85% of bytecodeLimit`. Counts go to two shared-array slots
  and are printed first in the Archon's indicator string (indicator strings truncate silently at
  an undocumented length — put the important fields first). Wrapped in its own try/catch.
- Comms: shared array as a fixed-slot message bus (`SA_*` constants, lines 102-146): packed
  location `1 + x*64 + y` (0 = empty), per-purpose slots with one writer discipline and an explicit
  clear condition; `SA_STAMP` round stamp for per-round rollover; `SA_FOCUS` shared focus-fire
  target; enemy-structure slots cleared when a unit stands adjacent and sees nothing.
- Navigation (`moveToward`, ~line 1657): vendored unrolled within-vision Dijkstra (radius^2 <= 20,
  cost 1 + rubble; MIT, from jmerle/camel_case, itself from Malott Fat Cats 2021) returning the
  first step, with a `blockedDirection = lastDir.opposite()` anti-oscillation guard; if the step
  is blocked (usually by a friendly), try `rotateLeft/rotateRight` before falling back to an
  8-direction greedy scorer (distance, rubble penalty, momentum bonus, map-center tiebreak).
  `repositionForRubble`: after attacking, step to a lower-rubble tile still in range.
- Exploration (`moveExplore`, ~line 1699): persistent random heading, re-rolled 1/15 rounds or
  when blocked; stuck detector = anchor every 10 rounds, if moved < 2 tiles then random-walk for
  10 rounds.
- Micro: `betterTarget` (~line 1310) three-tier priority + lowest HP + distance-to-self tiebreak;
  retreat only at critical HP unless near home; group reinforcement toward `SA_FOCUS`.
- Symmetry: `armyObjective()` falls back to the point mirror of own Archon; explicit symmetry
  detection was tried and rejected (Iteration 88) — see gotchas.

### 5. Gotchas and process rules (BC22)

- Static fields are per-robot, not team-shared; a shared fixed-seed `Random` gives mirrored robots
  the same sequence (seed from `rc.getID()`).
- `senseNearbyRobots()` etc. return in engine scan order — "first match wins" is an absolute-
  position bias. Audit tie-breaks on day one; run mirror matches (bot vs byte-identical copy)
  periodically and on every tie-break change.
- Verify engine semantics from ground truth (`javap` on the jar, replay header stats, engine
  source), and sweep the full `RobotController` API for methods never called (found `mutate`,
  transform modes 80 iterations late).
- Diff runs game-by-game; one-directional or one-map-many-opponents diffs are real, scattered
  mixed-direction ones are noise. Always diff against the *current* baseline.
- Don't skip the full Gauntlet on a clean small sample for build/priority/threshold changes.
- Check how often a proposed trigger condition would fire across *other* recent games before
  building on it; check whether the feature's game state ever arises at all.
- Read vendored opponent source (BC22 allowed it) before hypothesizing about its doctrine.
- Archive one replay per iteration in the same commit; it lapsed for 14 iterations otherwise.
- Never idle: after 3 rejects in one area, change area or make a structural attempt.

## Season 2 — battlecode26-vibe

### 1. Training method and how well it worked

Started as a from-scratch rewrite of the BC22 loop (`TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md` lines 1-565, same
hyperparameters: WinPct 60, 10/10 hypothesis/solution tries, BenchmarkEvery 3, ReproSample 8,
near-miss 5/3, MaxConsecutiveRejects 3) plus game-specific sections (chirality, backstab policy,
NPC cats, synthetic policy archetypes as opponents). The second half of the file (lines 273-933)
is what the season actually learned, and it substantially changes the method:
- **Instruments ranked by resolution** (line 273): mirror vs latest snapshot (50% by construction)
  is the primary accept test; peers are a regression check; `vs_old_bots` and benchmarks (~3-6%)
  are direction only. But resolution != representativeness: the mirror can prove a feature isn't
  paying for itself, never that it is unnecessary (trap ring ablation: mirror +7.4%, benchmark
  early-wipes 16% -> 26%).
- **Three cheap checks** before interpreting any run: arm-to-arm identity (`same == n` means the
  change never executed — caught Iterations 66/68/70), normalise counters per round, correlate
  timestamps before attributing a cost.
- **Dose-response** instead of re-running (the Gauntlet is deterministic): always measure the zero
  arm; an inverted high dose rejects that dose, not the mechanism; a concave curve is stronger
  evidence than any single point.
- **Ablate accepted features** when stalled: six ablations in one day found the two features
  carrying nearly all value (exploration-heading reassignment ~+28 pts, `REPLACEMENT_RESERVE`
  ~+24) and one accepted at "95.0%" worth ~0.
- **Reachability check** before a hypothesis (line 567): is the branch ever taken? Cheese/vision/
  counter gates that are dead by round 150 sank Iterations 137/139/141.
- **Measure the shared pool** when changing who spends a capped team resource (line 612).
- **`vs_old_bots` as a pre-accept gate** when margins are thin (line 657): caught a +1/+1 accept
  that was -10 games on the old-bot roster.
- **Post-accept routine** (line 693): four scripts, archive a replay, commit explicit paths.
- **Swept-map score** (maps won from *both* sides) because on a fully-split archetype 100% of the
  result is spawn side (Iteration 226); `paired-check.sh` (two maps of opposite character,
  control re-measured, before any Gauntlet) after Iterations 232/243/247.
- **Closed directions list** (line 807) with the measured reason for each closure; re-opening
  needs a new reason bearing on that cause (3 re-opens: 1 accepted, 2 firmly re-rejected).
- Benchmark rule (`BENCHMARK.md`): five real 2026 tournament bots vendored; **source never read**
  (clone/compile/run/replay-analysis only; package renames by `sed` without displaying contents).

Results:
- ~252 iterations logged, 34 accepted snapshots (`src/g_iter1..g_iter34`) => ~13.5% accept rate
  (headings: 35 accepted, 121 rejected, 19 void/inert; many "closed without a run").
- Final (Iteration 251, `TRAINING_LOG.md` tail): peers 162/216 (75%), swept-wins 60/108; head-to-
  head vs previous snapshot 29/54 (53.7%); benchmarks 10/162 (6.2%) vs `bench_finalist`/
  `bench_spaark`/`bench_stroke`/`bench_lecture`/`bench_anicolao`; `vs_old_bots`
  (`progress/vs_old_bots_history.csv`): 100% vs g_iter1, 90.7% vs g_iter21, 74.1% vs g_iter31.
- Benchmark smoke test (log line 3373): real tournament bots killed our King in 21-46 rounds; in
  the first 60 rounds they used 14-27 traps, 19-71 ratnaps, 9-36 throws vs our 0/0/0. The
  session's own verdict (line 6613): "No bot improvement. The output is methodological."
- Benchmark ceiling diagnosed as structural (Iterations 195/238): enemy King HP at our death was
  600 in 7 of 8 losses; per-rat action rate equal to a finalist's (10.3% vs 9.0%) but half the
  rat-turns (2104 vs 4187) because their rats survive.

### 2. What worked / what did not

Worked (with measurements):
1. Exploration heading committed per robot from `rc.getID()` and **reassigned only after repeated
   exploration-specific stalls** (Iteration 32; ablation 22.2% => ~+28 pts, the largest effect
   measured). A shared stuck counter as the trigger regressed 70% -> 57.5%.
2. `REPLACEMENT_RESERVE` — hold cheese to rebuild a collapsed army (Iteration 39, 90.0%, largest
   jump; ablation ~+24). Then re-denominated in rats (`8 * getCurrentRatCost()`, Iteration 251):
   concave dose curve 158/159/162/159, accepted with every instrument non-negative.
3. Emergency build override when the army is gone (Iteration 40) — worth ~0 on the mirror but 15
   peer games (Iteration 175); only the peer audit revealed it.
4. Bug2 wall-following for *fixed* targets only (Iteration 35; ablation +5.6). Applied to moving
   targets it regressed 75% -> 65%.
5. Fixing seven `d < bestDist` scans (Iteration 41): side A 85% / side B 40% became balanced; one
   `betterTarget(d, bestDist)` helper with per-robot RNG tiebreak.
6. Static defensive ring around the King (Iteration 48/96): 0 wins but survival 15-38x; restoring
   it halved benchmark early wipes 26% -> 13%; the ring armed only when attacked (Iteration 211).
7. Revoking the "desperate -> bite first" licence (Iteration 210): we had been starting our own
   backstabs; found by tracing King losses.
8. Reactive cat trap by the immobile King (Iteration 128) — the one unit for whom the alternative
   action is worth less; the same action on mobile rats lost every time (130/134/135).
9. Halving `BUILD_WINDOW_ROUNDS` 400 -> 200 (Iteration 240; 5 gained : 0 lost, directional).
10. Instrument repairs counted as wins: benchmark suite, `resync_archetypes.py`, swept-map score,
    exception counter in `gauntlet.sh`, `paired-check.sh`, `early_wipes.py`.

Did not work (with measurements):
1. Eight `MAX_POPULATION` raises / production increases (28-31, 34, 37, 60, 125, 163, 219): boom-
   bust, or the King starves; "more rats" loses because rat cost scales with population.
2. "Damage does not convert" — five mechanism-verified damage increases (142/147/158/179/180/190)
   moved nothing; trade ratio 7 kills : 347 losses.
3. Any cross-unit coordination: focus fire (158, void — 90-degree cone means one rat rarely sees
   two targets), rotation sweeps (152), squeak steering/luring (109/110/166/170/237), broadcast
   bearings (184). `RESEARCH.md` section 7: "Distrust anything requiring cross-unit coordination."
4. Multi-King formation (18, 50, 74, 75 at 0/162, 106, 107, 146, 173 at -28 peers).
5. Raids on the enemy King (79-81; our rats within range 0 of 3335 sampled rounds).
6. Unconditional kiting (Iteration 42) — the premise (attack every round, move every other) did
   not hold in this ruleset; check the cooldown arithmetic before importing a micro technique.
7. Survival bought with inactivity (Iteration 191: deaths halved, -18 peer games; 225: -23).
8. Archetype staleness (three times): peers built from `src/bot` drifted, inflating 62.5% to
   "95.0%"; later the peers converged onto us and became mirrors with zero information (222/226).
9. Accepting on a lopsided instrument (Iteration 63: benchmarks +2, peers 38%, mirror 33.3% ->
   retracted); rejecting on one (Iteration 78 -> re-tested as 88, +4 mirror, accepted).
10. Single-map mechanism checks and quoted controls: Iteration 232 flipped LOSS->WIN on a sparse
    maze and scored 130/216 vs 161 baseline; Iteration 247 quoted a stale "r1032" control that
    the current build already beat; Iteration 251 first diffed against a rejected candidate
    mistaken for the baseline (same headline score).

ReplayDump FlatBuffers note (why it differs from 2022 and why 2024 should follow 2022 instead):
BC26 moved actions into a per-`Turn` **union vector** whose members are partly `Struct`s; the
generated `Turn.actions(Table, j)` accessor cannot return a `Struct`, so the tool lives in
`package com.google.flatbuffers` to reach `Table.__offset(26) / __vector / __vector_len /
__indirect` and `__init()`s each member at its byte offset (vtable slot 26 = 0-based field 11
because a union occupies two slots). It also found the engine packing `numRatKings + 10*cheese`
into `teamAliveRatKings`. The 2024 format is the older parallel-vector layout, so the BC22
Python reader is the right base; keep BC26's habits (tests that pin decoding constants, unique
temp dirs, hard-error on unknown flags, labels accumulated over all rounds not just the window).

### 4. Bot architecture patterns worth reusing (`src/bot/RobotPlayer.java`, 1531 lines)

- `run()` (line 52): `rng = new Random(rc.getID())`; per-turn `roundAtTurnStart`; dispatch by unit
  type; `finally { reportBytecodeBudget(rc, roundAtTurnStart); Clock.yield(); }`.
  `reportBytecodeBudget` (line 1521): same overrun/near-miss (90%) test, printed as
  `bytecode used/limit (status)` in every non-producer's indicator string. Measured usage was ~4%
  of budget all season — the 2026 constraint was elsewhere, but the monitor cost nothing.
- Navigation (line 1179-1330): Bug2 with `bugTarget/bugClosestDistSq/bugRoundsFollowing`,
  rotation direction from `rc.getID() % 2`, state reset on target change, direct move always
  attempted when not tracing, leave wall-following only when strictly closer than ever; scoped to
  fixed targets (`useBugNav`), greedy `tryMove` + 2-tile oscillation escape for moving targets;
  `digTowardOpenSpace` fallback when fully blocked.
- Exploration (line 1460): personal heading from `ALL_DIRECTIONS[id % 8]` (an explicit 8-array —
  `Direction.allDirections()` includes CENTER, which silently no-op'd 1 in 9 robots), dedicated
  explore-call position history, reassignment after 2 consecutive stalls.
- Comms (lines 77-127, 1018): producer writes home location (x+1, y+1) and a guessed enemy
  location to fixed slots; consumers read them. Only producers could write in 2026; rats had a
  broadcast (`squeak`) that never paid off.
- Symmetry: never inferred in 2026 (`RESEARCH.md` calls this the biggest unexploited "Battlecode
  101" item); the mirror-match package `src/mirror_check/` and the Iteration 222 audit procedure
  (27 maps x both sides vs a byte-identical copy; expect 50.0% and a side-mirrored A/B split) are
  the reusable parts.
- Peer archetypes as measuring instruments (`src/pure_cooperator`, `immediate_defector`,
  `opportunistic`, `rusher`): worked only for the archetype that differs **early and
  structurally** (`immediate_defector`: 24/27 swept maps); late-diverging or converged ones are
  mirrors.

### 5. Gotchas and process rules (BC26)

- Peers derived from your own bot must be rebuilt mechanically from the newest *accepted*
  snapshot, checked automatically in the runner, and are worthless once they no longer differ
  early in the game.
- A game count is contaminated by spawn side; report swept maps and the A/B split.
- Determinism: identical code gives byte-identical results (verified for peers and benchmarks),
  so re-running is zero information; vary the dose, measure the zero arm, compare win *sets*
  (Iterations 45/46: identical 29/54 totals with 9 discordant games each way => noise floor
  about +/-9 games in 54).
- Sample wins and mirrors, not only losses: "our share of the biggest score term is low in our
  losses" is circular (five wasted iterations, 217-221).
- Read the guard you nest a new condition inside (132/139), and check the branch is reachable
  with the treasury/vision/counter numbers already in the log.
- Count exceptions per game in the runner; a per-turn catch turns a missing `canSense` guard into
  a "failed strategy".
- When a change alters who draws on a capped team resource, instrument the pool first.
- Prefer capability-preserving, zero-marginal-cost changes; the three that ever paid in 2026 were
  a standing defence, spending hoarded resource, and removing pure waste.
- Vendored benchmark source is never read; only their replays are.
- Commit explicit paths right after snapshotting; `track_vs_old_bots.py` labels a row by the
  highest `g_iterN` on disk at run time, so append only when the tree matches.

## Day-one rules for battlecode24-vibe (merged from both seasons)

1. Iteration 0 ships with: bytecode overrun/near-miss monitor in `finally`, per-robot RNG seeded
   from `rc.getID()`, a single `betterTarget`-style tie-break helper used at every selection site,
   no fixed `Direction[]` orderings, no `Team.A/B` references, and an indicator-string layout with
   the diagnostic counters first.
2. Tooling before strategy: parallel bare-JVM runner with `results.csv` (+ win type, exception
   count, swept maps), snapshot script, game-by-game diff tool, Python replay reader regenerated
   from the 2024 `.fbs` with a synthetic-replay test suite, `--metrics` CSV, per-robot trace and
   local-terrain flags, mirror-check package, fixed old-bot roster CSV + charts.
3. Accept only on an even instrument (head-to-head vs latest snapshot and swept maps), confirm on
   the lopsided ones (benchmarks, old-bot roster) that it is not a regression, and never accept a
   marginal result without a dose curve or a directional flip pattern.
4. Trace first, then hypothesise; pre-register variables; check reachability, trigger frequency
   across other games, and whether the feature's game state ever arises.
5. Diff against the current baseline only; verify that the control run *is* the baseline
   (timestamps, not headline score).
6. Vendor real 2024 bots as benchmarks without reading source; note their tactics from replays.
7. Keep a closed-directions list with measured reasons; re-open only on a changed cause.
8. Log areas attempted; after three rejects in an area, change area or make a structural swing.
9. Archive one replay and regenerate charts in the accept commit; stage explicit paths.
10. Reread `RESEARCH.md` (both years) when stalled: infer map symmetry early, hybrid bug/greedy
    navigation, comms schema before logic, micro over macro, distrust coordination-heavy
    features, rewrite rather than retrofit when the strategy has stopped moving.
