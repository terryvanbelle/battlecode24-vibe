# Lens traps

## Against the upper tier, the stun gap is volume. Stun technique is at parity stun for stun, and we lead the rest of the band. (strong)

Evidence: 720-game g_iter7 control (36 games per bot, 180 upper-tier). A scratch replay reader on the VM (~/txscratch/tx/Tx.java, 371k traps) gives one row per trap: build geometry, trigger, victims, and follow-up over 4 rounds. Post-setup stuns per game, ours vs theirs (upper): 188+-6 vs 464+-16. One-step stuns (an enemy within dist2 8 at build): 94+-4 vs 335+-12. Per 100 rounds: r201-400 23.4 vs 44.4, r401-1000 11.3 vs 29.9, r1001+ 12.8 vs 33.9. Victims per game: 790 vs 2,204. Per one-step stun: 5.45 vs 5.41 victims, median latency 4 vs 3 rounds, 97% vs 96% triggered. Against the rest of the band we build more: 213+-5 vs 166+-6.

Confound: Volume follows crumbs, and crumbs follow who wins the fight (next finding).

## Kill rewards account for the whole crumb gap. The rest of the volume gap is price: the upper tier pays about 16% less per stun through build-6 builders, a family that is parked. Our bank is pinned at the trap reserve all game. (strong)

Evidence: Census L1-L3, upper-tier games, income per game: start plus passive 16.1k for both sides; map crumbs 7.2k vs 7.5k; kill rewards 2.6k (85 paid kills) vs 14.8k (492 of our 764 deaths fall on our territory). Totals 25.9k vs 38.3k. One-step stun cost proxy (nearest own robot's build level): 96.5 vs 80.9 crumbs. Their nearest builder is at build 6 for 35% of one-step stuns, ours for 1%; 77% of ours come from build-0 robots. Rest of band: 95.8 vs 93.7, 6% at build 6. Our median bank is 230 at r200 and 210-240 at every checkpoint to r1000. The upper tier's median: 2,645 at r200, 2,557 at r250, 1,450 at r300, 437 at r400.

Confound: Kill rewards are an outcome of the fight and of where it happens (T15 territory micro is closed). Cheaper stuns need concentrated build XP (the builders family is parked).

## Our step-in strikes trigger their stuns, and about 41% of our deaths against the upper tier follow one of them. (strong)

Evidence: 360-game subset, ~/txscratch/tx4: 73,243 of their stun triggers. On the triggering move, 88% of our robots advance toward them, 66% strike that turn (62% do both), and 90% carry the 'fight' note. When they trigger ours, 86% advance and 45% strike. Their builders back off after building: +0.31 tiles over 2 rounds, 48% move away and 33% toward; ours +0.05, 42% and 41%. Deaths within 4 rounds of being caught in an enemy stun: ours 41% vs theirs 22% (upper), 30% vs 23% (rest). 32% of our deaths are frozen at r-1, 15% of theirs.

Confound: Traps are invisible. Step-in discipline (ENGAGE_HP) and stun-wary avoidance are already parked or closed, so this explains the deaths but offers no new handle.

## Follow-up per opportunity, clumping and the response to being stunned are all at parity. What we lack is the number of our robots near our stun when it fires, which is an outcome of the fight. (strong)

Evidence: ~/txscratch/tx2, 293,699 triggers. Our robots within dist2 20 when our stun fires: 5.11 vs 6.76 of theirs when theirs fires. Ready turns in rounds r+1..r+3 per trigger: 8.8 vs 12.2. On a ready turn one step from a frozen victim we strike a victim 0.53 of the time, they 0.50. Hits per victim: 0.85 vs 0.95. Victims dead within 4 rounds, per-game mean: 0.144+-0.005 vs 0.172+-0.004 (within-game difference -0.028+-0.006); against the rest +0.082+-0.004 in our favour. Net trade per trigger (victims killed minus the stunning team's deaths within dist2 20 over 4 rounds): +0.07+-0.03 for our stuns vs +0.50+-0.02 for theirs; against the rest it reverses (+0.52 vs -0.04). Kills per trigger rise with our support (0.17 to 1.30), but against the upper tier our own nearby deaths rise in step, so the net stays near 0 at every support level. Robots in contact have 6.46 vs 6.53 allies within dist2 13. When stunned, the victims' unfrozen neighbours attack 0.22 vs 0.21, heal 0.25 vs 0.26, and do nothing 0.53 vs 0.53.

Confound: Support and net trade show who holds the local fight. Correlation only.

## In two states we leave frozen enemies unstruck far more often than the upper tier does: when the 'strong' test counts frozen enemies, and when our robot is below RETREAT_HP. (moderate)

Evidence: ~/txscratch/tx3, 452 games, ready turns one step from a frozen victim. 9.3% are blocked only because 'strong' counts the frozen enemies: we strike 0.12 of the time, the upper tier 0.37 in the same state. 7.2% are robots below RETREAT_HP 300: 0.05 vs 0.29. Turns where no unfrozen enemy threatens the reaching tile: 66 a game against the upper tier (39 + 27) and 58 against the rest. A further 20% of these turns are blocked because our own robots occupy the reaching tiles; that is saturation and cannot be fixed.

Confound: States are rebuilt from end-of-round positions, and tile occupancy changes during the round.

## Counter-stuns are our best stuns: a stun built beside an enemy stun that just fired, so it catches the robots stepping in to strike our frozen ones. We build them less than half as often as the upper tier. (moderate)

Evidence: A counter-stun here is built within dist2 25 and 0-3 rounds after an enemy stun fires. Ours average 6.19 victims and 0.82 kills per trigger, against 4.49 and 0.64 for all our stuns (upper); against the rest, 5.35 and 1.09 vs 4.48 and 0.87. Counter rate within 2 rounds: we answer 6.3% of their triggers, they answer 15.5% of ours (upper); 9.2% vs 8.4% against the rest. After a counter-stun, the original stunning team's deaths near the trap double (0.36 to 0.79 per trigger); the original victims' death rate does not change (0.184 vs 0.192). Our 300-crumb field-stun gate sits above our median bank of about 210.

Confound: Counter-stuns happen in bigger fights (stunning team's robots within dist2 13: 7.3 vs 5.0). Correlation only.

## Early stuns barely kill, while explosives kill in every phase and with little support. Even so, an explosive-for-stun swap is not worth an arm. (moderate)

Evidence: r201-400 against the upper tier: our field-stun victims die 5.2% of the time (0.27 kills per trigger), theirs 11.7% (0.64); after r600 ours die 14.7-15.2%. Explosive victims die 64-70% (ours, 1.6-1.9 kills per trigger) and 49-54% (theirs) in every period. Our explosive kill rate is 0.63 with 0-1 allies near and 0.80 with 7 or more; for stuns it is 0.05 vs 0.19. Against that: at our pinned bank a 400-crumb explosive gate rarely fires; the swap gives up a 3-turn freeze of about 5 enemies; and a one-tile trigger is slow (their field explosives fire after a median 36-49 rounds). The swap breaks even only at a trigger rate of 70% or more.

Confound: Kills within 4 rounds of a trap are not all caused by it.

## Our setup traps cost about the same per kill as early field stuns, so banking through setup (T5) gets no support here. (moderate)

Evidence: Setup traps per game against the upper tier: 25.6 stuns (17.5 ring, 8.9 dam) and 9.6 explosives, against their 2.6 stuns. Dam stuns fire at a median r291 (99% fire), catch 4.44 victims, and 6% of victims die. Ring stuns: median r387, 3.17 victims, 17% die. Setup explosives: median r524, 20% never fire (47% never against the rest), 2.49 victims, 68% die. That is about 24 kills a game for about 4,300 crumbs, roughly 180 crumbs per kill. Early field stuns against the upper tier cost about 385 crumbs per kill.

Confound: Kill counts miss the setup traps' flag-deterrence value; the closed ring-reserve arm showed that rings deter grabs.

## Negative result: the upper tier's water-trap flag moats leave our carriers stuck but do not change our capture conversion. (moderate)

Evidence: Strequals keeps a water trap on its flag tile: 5.4 a game, 177 of 195 on the tile, 49% rebuilt on the same tile, 85% triggered, and 84% of triggers have our carrier within dist2 9. andli28 builds 4.4 a game at dist2 1-2 from its homes, 68% in setup. Our moated carriers move 1.08 tiles in 10 rounds vs 2.72 unmoated. Conversion: 0.107+-0.022 when a moat fired (n=206) vs 0.120+-0.009 when nothing fired (n=1,405); Strequals 0.12 vs 0.09, andli28 0.08 vs 0.13. On our own side, the number of intact ring traps at an enemy grab does not change its conversion (0.21 with none intact vs 0.19-0.22 otherwise).

Confound: A moat fires only when the carrier steps next to the flag, so moated grabs are a selected set.

## Negative result: neither side places traps on chokepoints. (moderate)

Evidence: Field stuns on corridor tiles (at most 5 passable tiles in the 3x3): 6% of ours, 5% of theirs (upper). Corridor stuns fire more slowly (median 21 rounds vs 6). Explosives show the same pattern. Mean passable tiles in the 3x3: 8.0 vs 8.1; walls among the 8 neighbours: 0.67 vs 0.58.

Confound: The terrain measure is crude.

## Levers

- **C.STUN_PRESS (exploit our own fired stuns)**: Off by default, so g_iter7 plays byte-identical. Slots 37-44 (the AUC range, never written in g_iter7) hold a ring of our combat, carrier and counter stun locations: each build writes x*64+y+1 into the next slot. A robot that sees one of these tiles in vision with senseMapInfo(loc).getTrapType() != STUN stamps bits [15..13] with (round%7)+1. Each turn, at most 8 senseMapInfo calls, a robot marks every enemy within dist2 13 of a slot stamped in rounds r-2..r as frozen. Changes in Micro.fight: the 'strong' count (nearEnemies) and threat(l) count only unfrozen enemies; a robot below RETREAT_HP may take the engage branch for a tile that reaches a frozen enemy with no unfrozen enemy within dist2 10. bestTarget, HEAL_HOLD, retreat and carrier logic are unchanged. Indicator counter 'sp'. - **C.BUILD_ROUTE: price lever from the andli28 study, flagged as overlapping the parked builders family**: Not new to this lens: the andli28 study proposed C.BUILD_ROUTE but never built it.
